STARKNELL

Every jump ages your captains.
Time is the only currency.

A dark retro-CRT roguelike autobattler. You command a doomed deep-space expedition through the green-phosphor terminal of its own mainframe — routing a fleet across a branching sector chart, reading the casualty telegrams, and spending the only resource that matters. Every jump and every engagement ages every captain by a year or more, a whole lifespan is consumed inside a single run, and old age kills mid-run. Death — by age or by fire — is permanent. The one way to cheat the clock is a forbidden cybernetic conversion whose true cost stays redacted until it isn't.

▶ THE LOOP

HOW IT PLAYS

A run takes ten to twenty-five minutes and ends one of two ways. You assemble a fleet, pick a route, commit to a battle you cannot micromanage, and pay for all of it in years off your captains' lives. An adaptive darksynth score follows you down.

  • THE CLOCK

    TIME IS THE ENEMY

    Every jump and every engagement ages every captain by a year or more. A whole lifespan is consumed inside a single run, so old age is not a distant threat — it kills mid-run, on the chart, between battles. Death by age and death by fire are equally permanent.

  • THE FLEET

    ROWS, NOT UNITS

    Assemble a fleet under a command-capacity budget — every hull you take is a hull you didn't. Set your rows before you commit: vanguard draws fire, screen intercepts, rear stays protected. The battle honours that arrangement exactly.

  • THE BATTLE

    COMMAND, DON'T PILOT

    Combat resolves in real time — full-colour pixel art under a CRT shader, on a deterministic seeded simulation. You intervene only through captain abilities, at the moment you judge is the moment. No unit micro. No retreat.

  • THE SECTOR

    MANY NODES ARE NOT BATTLES

    Anomalies, derelicts, distress signals: branching text encounters with uncertain outcomes, and multi-step quest chains that resolve jumps later, long after you've forgotten what you agreed to. What you choose persists.

  • THE TEMPTATION

    THE CONTINUANCE PROTOCOL

    A recruited Oracle hull can research the one technology that cheats the clock: a cybernetic conversion worth twelve years, carrying one concealed side-effect per procedure — revealed only after it has been applied. At the sector's end, the Provenance Ark reads your crew manifest for the oldest name.

  • BETWEEN RUNS

    VARIETY, NEVER POWER

    Only unlocks persist — new hulls, captain archetypes, encounter packs that can appear. No fleet, no captains and no banked salvage cross the gap; every run starts fresh. Standing doctrine is a shape you chose, not a checklist you filled. The memorial wall remembers everyone.

▶ ONE CODEBASE

WHERE IT RUNS

Built with Flutter and Flame: one codebase across iOS, Android, Windows, macOS and the browser. The simulation is pure Dart and deterministic — bit-identical on a phone and in a browser tab — which is why the same seed produces the same expedition everywhere.

  • PLATFORMS

    DESKTOP, POCKET, BROWSER

    Windows and macOS on Steam. iPhone, iPad and Android phones and tablets on their own stores, in portrait or landscape. And a capped web demo that runs in a browser tab with nothing to install.

  • THE TERMINAL

    A CRT YOU CAN TUNE

    The whole interface is the expedition mainframe: green phosphor, scanlines, curvature and teletype. The full-screen CRT shader has lite and off modes and degrades itself automatically on hardware that can't carry it, so the effect never costs you the game.

▶ ON THE RECORD

IT COLLECTS NOTHING

STARKNELL has no server, no account, no analytics and no crash reporter, and it makes no network requests at all. Its Android manifest declares not one permission — not even INTERNET — and its iOS property list declares no usage strings, so there is nothing for the app to ask you for. Your expedition, your unlocks and your settings are written to your own device's storage and stay there; deleting the app deletes all of it. Purchases are handled by the store you bought from, and we never see payment details. The full, plainly-worded details — including how to erase every byte it stored — live on the privacy page.
