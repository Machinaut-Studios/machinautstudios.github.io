▶ COMING SOON  //  iOS + ANDROID

Widgetastrophe

Ship first.
Panic later.

Bolt features onto the conveyor to make every widget worth more — and every feature you bolt on is a new way for the widget to catch fire, launch across the room, fill with live bees or walk off the job. Nobody knows what a Widget is. It sells anyway. Safety machines answer each of those disasters, but a safety machine takes a belt tile that could have been earning. Rent comes due every 45 seconds and goes up by about a third every day.

▶ THE LOOP

HOW IT PLAYS

The features that sell best are the ones that catch fire.

Widgets ride the belt left to right and sell at the end. Every machine you bolt onto a tile adds dollars to every widget that passes it — and hands that widget one new way to go wrong. You are not fighting the factory. You are deciding how much of it you can afford to have on fire at once.

  • THE BELT

    TILES ARE THE BUDGET

    A shift starts with a short conveyor and a little cash. You buy more tiles, and you bolt a machine onto a tile. Every tile you spend on not going wrong is a tile that isn't making money, and that trade is the whole game.

  • FEATURES

    MORE IS WORSE, PROFITABLY

    Each feature machine adds its dollars to every widget that passes it, and its risk to every widget that passes it. Chrome is harmless and pays $6. A Free Will Module pays $160 and the widget starts having opinions.

  • THE INCIDENTS

    SEVEN WAYS TO LOSE ONE

    Glitter gunks the belt. Bevels slice it. Airbags, springs and rockets launch the widget across the room, where it clangs off your other machines. Lithium and smart chips set the tile alight and fire spreads. Bees escape and shut a machine down. Helium floats the widget up off the belt, and the ceiling keeps whatever reaches it. Free will walks it back to the intake.

  • YOUR HANDS

    TAP THE TROUBLE

    Tap a fire to fight it and hold to keep spraying. Tap a swarm to shoo it off the machine it's sitting on. Tap a floater to pop it before the ceiling gets it. And a mutinous widget takes three pep talks before it turns around and goes back to work.

  • THE CLOCK

    RENT IS DUE

    A day is 45 seconds long and it ends with rent. Rent grows by about a third every day, so a factory that was comfortable on day three is bankrupt on day seven unless it grew too. Growth is the only stable state.

  • GOING BROKE

    A SLIGHTLY CHEAPER YOU

    Miss rent and the board replaces you with a slightly cheaper you. The way out is a Bailout — half the next rent plus a cushion, bought with Bolts, or taken once a run for an opt-in video instead. The second Bailout in a run costs double the first.

▶ THREE WAYS IN

THE MODES

One is a campaign that introduces the disasters one at a time and then stops being polite. One takes the roster the campaign has unlocked and runs it until you go broke. The last is the same shift, with the same twist, for everybody on earth, once a day.

  • MODE 01

    40 CONTRACTS

    Five districts, eight contracts each: Widget Co. (est. this morning), The Fun Ones (features that deploy), Thermal Events (the brochure calls this warmth), Livestock (bees, obviously bees) and Free Will (the widget has decided otherwise). Each contract has a goal, a deadline and two bonus stars you will not get on the first go, and most of them add a rule twist on top.

  • MODE 02

    ENDLESS SHIFT

    Everything the campaign has unlocked, no goal, no deadline, and rent that keeps compounding. It ends when you can't make rent, because that is the only ending it has. Never interrupted by an ad — there is no cleared state for one to land on.

  • MODE 03

    DAILY SHIFT

    Five days, seeded from the date, identical on every device on earth — same widgets, same dice, same twist of the day (two of them every third day). Scored on sales. The streak counts days you played, not days you won, because showing up is the part we can fairly ask of you.

▶ THE HONEST BIT

WHAT IT COSTS

Widgetastrophe is free with ads. The ad rules below are conditions in the code, not intentions in a blog post: the placement cubit has exactly one placement, and it is the moment you leave a cleared contract's results card.

  • FREE  //  AD-SUPPORTED

    ADS, ON A LEASH

    A full-screen ad can appear only after a contract you cleared. Never after a loss, never after the Daily Shift, never in Endless, and never during play. Your first three cleared contracts are never interrupted at all, then it's at most every second clear, never inside two minutes of the last one, and four in a session is the hard stop.

  • ONE-TIME PURCHASE

    REMOVE ADS

    Buy it once and the between-contract ad never appears again. The opt-in video stays available, so you can still trade thirty seconds for a Bailout if you want one — you just never get shown an ad you didn't ask for.

  • THE HEX NUT

    BOLTS

    Bolts are earned by clearing contracts, turning up for the Daily and surviving Endless days. They buy Bailouts, Quiet Hours (a day with no interruption ads) and factory palettes — Day Shift and Night Shift are free, Blueprint, Bubblegum Plant, Rustbelt and Mint Condition are 500 each. Bolt packs exist for anyone who would rather buy the nuts than earn them.

A Bailout is the revive, and it is the one thing money touches that isn't cosmetic — which is why it is also available once a run for an opt-in video, at no cost, in every mode. Palettes repaint the factory and change nothing about what a widget is worth.

▶ ON THE RECORD

YOUR DATA STAYS YOURS

There is no account, no sign-in and no server of ours to talk to. The whole game is a simulation running on your own device, in portrait or landscape, on a plane if you like — progress, Bolts, stats, streaks and saved shifts sit in your phone's own storage and go when the app goes. It asks for no camera, no microphone, no location and no contacts. Two things reach the network at all: Google's ad network, which serves the ads, and the App Store or Google Play, which handle purchases — we never see payment details. The full, plainly-worded version lives on the privacy page, the same one we file with both stores.
